Timestral vs Laplace

Mistral AI · Timestral 209M code Apache-2.0; weights Modified MIT zero-shot one-step change series

Timestral is Mistral's zero-shot time-series model, released 15 October 2026 in four sizes; it emits 21 non-crossing quantiles per step, from the 1st to the 99th percentile. Run zero-shot on CPU with a fixed 128-length context, symmetric averaging and the nonnegativity clip switched off (the series are signed changes). The headline row is the 209M checkpoint scored on all 21 native levels; the other sizes and the nine-decile reconstruction are tabled below.

Resources: GitHub · Model card

What this page measures. One narrow thing: the sharpness and calibration of Timestral's univariate predictive density, one step ahead on a change series, scored against Laplace on identical windows. It does not exercise the model's covariates, multivariate inputs, long horizons, or the native quantile and path outputs it was built for, and its log score passes through a reconstruction of the model's quantiles (see the table note). A model can trail here and still be the right tool for the job it was designed for.

Live snapshot  Derived from the week-long round-robin study; counts grow as coverage deepens. Everything scores against Laplace on the identical series and windows.

Standalone

The model's own predictive, run zero-shot, scored per series against Laplace. Win, draw and loss are defined per series, as follows. Each series has a rolling test window of 64 one-step-ahead points; both forecasters are scored on the same points. For each point take the difference of log predictive densities, dt = log pmodel(yt) − log pLaplace(yt), with each log density floored at −20 first. The series' mean d̄ is tested against zero by a Diebold–Mariano test: the standard error is Newey–West with a Bartlett kernel and lag round(n1/3). The model wins the series if d̄ > 1.96·SE, loses it if d̄ < −1.96·SE, and otherwise the series is a draw; a series with fewer than eight finite differences is a draw. A draw means the 64 points cannot separate the two at the 5% level, not that they are equal, so with this window length wins and losses both understate the true rates and the median ΔLL is the better size estimate. Counts are over the series both arms cover.

Star plot

Timestral standalone against Laplace, on the same six regime axes as the site's standalone radar. Each radius is the log-likelihood win rate, (wins + ½·ties) / n, divided by one half so an even split with Laplace sits on the dashed 1.0 ring; outward beats Laplace more often, inward less. It is a share of series, not a ratio of likelihoods. The solid polygon is the headline arm; the dashed grey one, where present, is the tight nine-decile reconstruction. The M4-hourly set splits into soft and hard waveforms by corpus order, matching that radar.

Collaborative use

The recalibration (@lap) and portfolio (&lap) arms were built for the three strongest models first, so Timestral is scored standalone here. The sidecar wraps any per-step predictive, so these arms can be added without retraining. See the sidecar pattern.

Protocol

Fixed 128-length context, rolling one-step test window, no fitting, each model in its own environment. Strata split the cached FRED universe and the M4-hourly set by frequency and regime. Full method on the sidecar pattern page and in the methodology.

Architecture and methodology

Timestral is a patched transformer with an hourglass encoder, trained to emit 21 quantile levels per step with a context of up to 32,768 points and support for covariates and multiple variates, none of which this univariate study uses. Its 1% and 99% quantiles are native outputs, so its density needs no bandwidth inflation to carry tails: scored on all 21 levels the 123M and 209M are ahead of Laplace at the median on weekly, monthly and M4-hourly, level on price and a few hundredths behind on daily econ, with matching coverage, while on the nine deciles alone it reads like every other quantile model, about half a nat behind. Pretraining contamination is unchecked.
