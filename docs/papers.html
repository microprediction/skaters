Transforms all the way down: automatic online distributional forecasting by conjugation

The methods paper. Every prediction is a full predictive distribution, assembled by composition: invertible transforms chain onto a single distributional leaf fitted by a proper scoring rule, and the collection collapses into one forecast function with no exposed tuning parameters. It leads the held-out log-likelihood race against classical, neural, and foundation-model baselines on FRED series, with the asset-price split reported rather than averaged away. Written twice, in pure Python and zero-dependency JavaScript, held to 1e-6 agreement by a parity suite.

A stronger univariate benchmark for foundational time-series model evaluation

Reported progress for foundation time-series models rests on comparisons that leave out a strong classical reference. Across eleven benchmark papers the classical arms are per-window refits of ETS, ARIMA and Theta; no online forecaster appears, and no suite scores a full predictive density by log score. This paper supplies the missing reference, scored one step ahead on held-out log density over an 19,940-series panel. The comparison is decided by a step the suites leave unstated: how the model's nine quantiles become a density. Under a tight reconstruction the reference leads TimesFM 3.0 by half a nat on the median series of every stratum; under a generous one the two are level at the median, and the reference keeps the better coverage. Every number derives from a version-controlled store; the verifier and a three-tier reproduction guide live next to the paper.

On the importance of evaluating foundational time-series models one step ahead in an investment context

A technical note. Under log utility, an investor who can trade every interval chooses today's position from the distribution of the next return alone, and the regret from trading on a forecast is bounded by its excess expected log loss. Scores pooled over long horizons therefore do not establish superiority for that decision. The note recommends reporting one step ahead on its own.

Two ways to correct a predictive

A research note. Recalibration delivers nominal coverage and costs sharpness; a conditional reparameterisation of laplace's residual scale gains a little and moves coverage toward nominal. The two are case studies on different forecasts, panels and scores, not a paired comparison.

An empirical study of the conformal information gap

The companion study, with skaters as the measurement instrument. Under logarithmic loss the irreducible cost of a retained representation is a conditional mutual information, the information gap. A nested ladder of transforms prices each representation family prequentially on 701 economic series: conditional scale is the largest and most reliable component of the gain, and scale-normalized empirical pooling beats raw pooling on 87 percent of series under CRPS.
